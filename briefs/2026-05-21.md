- NVIDIA delivered another large beat and guided above expectations, but the stock still fell on the day, which tells us AI fundamentals remain strong while expectations remain extremely high.

## 4. AI Sector Radar

- Compute and hyperscaler demand:
  NVIDIA reported fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of `$81.6 billion` and Data Center revenue of `$75.2 billion`, then guided Q2 revenue to about `$91 billion`. The key insight is that demand is broadening beyond the big hyperscalers into AI-native cloud customers.
- Memory and HBM:
  Micron and SK hynix both remain tightly tied to the Vera Rubin cycle. This keeps the HBM story alive and supports the idea that memory pricing and allocation should remain favorable.
- Optics and networking:
  Lumentum, AAOI, and Coherent all have fresh evidence that the optical layer is moving from a concept trade into a production-ramp trade.
- Storage and inference:
  Sandisk's datacenter ramp and the Sandisk/SK hynix HBF initiative both point to a stronger inference-storage investment case. This matters because the next AI bottleneck may not just be compute or HBM, but also memory hierarchy and data movement.
- Power, cooling, and infrastructure:
  X-energy, Talen, and Dow are part of a growing cluster of names positioned around the AI power constraint.
- Physical AI and robotics:
  This was a major NVIDIA theme at GTC on `March 16, 2026`, but I did not verify it as a major theme in yesterday's earnings call. The investable takeaway is still useful: physical AI extends the demand map into industrial automation, edge compute, networking, sensors, and power.
